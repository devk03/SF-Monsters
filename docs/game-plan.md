# Proposed game plan

## Identity and gameplay

Working title: SF Mini Monsters.
Tone: an adult protagonist, SF satire, optional drunk/high character states,
weird dates, and extensive jokes and Easter eggs.

Explore, encounter mini monsters, recruit them, build a team, win turn-based
battles, and unlock neighborhoods. Familiar genre systems include levels,
transformations, elemental matchups, status effects, and championship progression.
Use a six-monster team and four equipped moves, with capture, levels,
evolution, abilities, held items, healing, and storage. Exact formulas remain open.
The catalog target is 150 collectible entries; evolution stages count separately.

All names and fictional concepts below are provisional.

## City geography

Compress the city while preserving the ocean to the west, bay to the east,
southern hills, and northwest parkland. Neighborhoods act as town hubs.
Parks, beaches, stairways, tunnels, and waterfronts act as routes or dungeons.

Golden Gate Park links Sunset, Haight, and Richmond. Presidio links Richmond
and Marina. Longer jumps such as Bernal–Dogpatch and SoMa–Fillmore use explicit
transit connections. Unlock shortcuts for revisits; the campaign order does
not imply literal adjacency.

## Marina nightlife

Fictional bars: The Quarter Zip, Liquidity Lounge, and The Last Round.
The mystery features identical vest owners, a bouncer demanding proof of
product-market fit, competitive trivia, and a creature in a karaoke speaker.
The reward connects the Palace of Fine Arts to the main story.
The player can order drinks and continue playing while the character is drunk.
Tenderloin has an optional high-character sequence. Both states change dialogue
and perception while keeping controls, menus, saving, and progression usable.
Optional weird dates provide branching conversations, rewards, and callbacks.

## Main story and antagonist

The player is a local courier who discovers creatures abandoning their habitats.
ClearSky, a fictional startup, promises permanently sunny weather. Its
installations destabilize neighborhood microclimates. Founder Arden Vale
insists every problem is a distribution issue.

Gym leaders are community stewards. Their challenges teach habitat systems;
their seals build the alliance needed to stop ClearSky.

Patch is the recurring rival and a ClearSky field operator. They interfere
with the player, uncover the harm, and can become an ally. This interprets
the requested side villain as an antagonist with an independent character arc.

## Legendary mini monster

Bayveil is a tiny floating manta made of fog, with kelp-like whiskers and
lighthouse eyes. Glimpses appear throughout the campaign. Restoring habitats
unlocks its recruitment quest after the main confrontation.

## Championship

Eight gym seals unlock a ferry to a fictional offshore sanctuary.
The Bay Council supplies the four-member championship:

| Member | Specialty |
| --- | --- |
| The Naturalist | Weather, habitats, and regeneration |
| The Engineer | Defensive combinations and machinery |
| The Performer | Tempo, disruption, and status effects |
| The Archivist | Adaptable teams representing city history |

The rival is the final championship opponent.

## Real-person casting

The user wants real SF tech people and recognizable public online characters
for gyms, villains, rivals, and ideally every interactable human NPC.
See [cast research](cast-research.md) for sourced public themes and proposed roles.
This supersedes the fictional-handle NPC direction. The earlier ClearSky/Arden/
Patch plot above is a legacy pitch pending replacement by the real-person cast.
Write original dialogue; game roles and neighborhood placements are fictional.
Posts appear on an offline in-game bulletin board with quest-dependent callbacks.

## Historical foundations

These verified historical hooks inspire fictional adventures:

- Golden Gate Park began on sand dunes: [SF Recreation and Parks](https://recpark.sf.gov/1119/History-of-Golden-Gate-Park).
- Sutro Baths had seven pools and tidal engineering: [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/goga/learn/historyculture/sutro-baths.htm).
- The 1915 exposition occupied today's Marina and Presidio bayfront: [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/prsf/learn/historyculture/1915-panama-pacific-international-exposition.htm).
- Cable-car invention and preservation inspire a machinery quest: [SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/cable-cars/cable-car-history).

Research further historical dialogue before treating it as fact. Handle
displacement, civil-rights history, and community struggles with care.

## Delivery stages

1. Prove the two-neighborhood playable slice and both target players.
2. Expand to 16 hubs, eight gyms, the Bay Council, villain arc, and Bayveil.
3. Balance and verify builds, document contributions, and prepare publication.

Initial full-campaign targets: 150 collectible monster entries and 8–12 hours.
These are planning targets that must be reassessed after the playable slice.
Multiplayer, live social feeds, and cloud accounts are outside the initial scope.
