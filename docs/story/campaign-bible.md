Date: October 16, 2026. Status: complete **proposed story treatment**, awaiting
editorial review. This is not a claim that maps, scenes or dialogue are implemented.
The user commissioned this writing pass for a contributor workshop. The eight gyms
remain required in the game; accepting a particular contributor assignment is optional.

Read with [map atlas](map-atlas.md), [gym briefs](gym-handoffs.md) and

## The game in one paragraph

An adult Sunset courier recovers a broken habitat sensor and accidentally becomes
the only person carrying observations between neighborhoods that have stopped
listening to each other. A fictional Overclock Collective promises every monster
its final evolution on the same night. Its demonstration works beautifully on a
dashboard because the dashboard treats sixteen very different habitats as one.
The courier and their companions collect live evidence, earn eight Field League
certifications, and assemble a way to let each neighborhood recover independently.
Roon must learn to make his discoveries useful; Beff must confront the creatures
his perfect demonstration cannot describe. The city survives Launch Night. Then
the player proves what their team has learned at the Treasure Island championship.

All public-person roles, schemes, battles and dialogue in this treatment are
invented game fiction. They are not accounts of real behavior or endorsement.

## What is fixed for this draft

- Keep the actual opening: Sunset → Muni → SoMa → Cognition badge → Mission.
- Recover a **sensor**, not an unseen stranded courier. The old rescue pitch is
  superseded. A delivery can matter without inventing a second rescue objective.
- Exactly sixteen adventure hubs; downtown office districts and Treasure Island
  are clearly named supporting areas, not extra towns or renamed neighborhoods.
- Eight startup gyms, geographically clustered where evidence warrants. Do not
  relocate offices to make every other neighborhood contain a gym.
- Stop Overclock before the championship. Sam is champion, not a surprise villain.
- Bayveil appears in the climax but recruitment is a separate, retriable postgame
  quest. Balaji's challenge is optional postgame, not a second mandatory ending.
- Optional apartment evidence, dates, intoxication, jokes and hidden discoveries
  never carry the only copy of a required clue or block the full collection.

## Why the player cares

The player is an adult local courier with an ordinary studio, delivery work and
an adopted field companion. No secret royal lineage, chosen-one power or forced
personal tragedy is needed. The first motivation is a paid job. The second is
the companion reacting badly to the same pulse that seems to be helping a demo.
The third is specific people remembering that the courier showed up when a
delivery, performance, garden or public service mattered.

Dialogue lets the player be earnest, dry or impatient. These choices change tone
and callbacks, not whether an otherwise viable team can finish the game.

## The habitat system: five rules

1. **Observe locally.** Fictional habitat relays read conditions such as fog,
   moisture, light and activity. They support places where monsters live; they do
   not create wildlife, control real SF weather or dictate anyone's personality.
2. **Evolution takes readiness.** Monsters grow through ordinary experience and
   care. The system can support recovery; pushing every habitat into the same
   growth conditions creates stress instead of universal improvement.
3. **A replay is not a fresh reading.** Overclock's scheduler replaces variable
   observations with a smooth demonstration trace. Early failures are mistaken
   for bad local hardware because the central display keeps reporting success.
4. **Local isolation is safe; a sudden citywide cutoff is not.** Relays can be
   put into supervised local mode. Shutting down the whole network while its
   habitats are depending on it would strand recovering monsters. This explains
   why one person cannot simply unplug the plot.
5. **No master creature fixes everything.** Bayveil is a rare fog-dwelling animal,
   not a magic administrator. Its changing pulse exposes a bad assumption; the
   people and monsters of the city still have to do the work.

This is fictional ecology. Historical plaques describe sourced SF history,
not evidence that real landmarks once contained our imaginary relay system.

## Why gyms and badges belong in the story

The fictional Field League is a voluntary training network hosted by eight startup
labs. Companies maintain different interface standards for the habitat equipment
they support. A badge certifies that a trainer can supervise companions around
one class of equipment; it does not appoint a company to govern a neighborhood.
Local staff restore immediate safety before a gym battle. Losing the optional-to-
attempt battle leaves the player able to heal and retry, not responsible for an
ongoing emergency. All eight victories remain required for the final supervised
switch-over and the championship.

The eight contributions are distinct: Cognition coordinates; Notion orders
dependencies; Midjourney separates appearance from measurement; Mercor assigns
specialists; Cursor rolls back a bad change; Perplexity verifies evidence;
Anthropic bounds recovery; OpenAI schedules limited resources. The player earns
competence and carries the combined plan. No contributor needs to invent a new
world-saving object to make their gym important.

## Act I — A delivery becomes evidence

### Outer Sunset: something is cached

O01–O04: Roon's phone message commissions sensor recovery. Karpathy supplies a
starter and sensible field advice. A wild monster guards the damaged sensor;
winning or recruiting it recovers the parcel, while losing or fleeing permits a
retry. The display flashes a fog signal that its smooth forecast cannot explain.
Roon pays the promised 500 coins and sends the courier to Scott in South Park.

Optional A01–A03: the studio window visibly contradicts SUNSHINE FOREVER on a
spare beacon. Correcting it reveals a quieter second pulse. This is corroboration
and an eventual callback, never a prerequisite to solving the main mystery.
The player can rest at home and refuse the correction without losing access.

### SoMa: a contained problem, then a challenge

O05–O09 / G01: Jonathan's Muni connection establishes long-distance travel.
The clinic, garden encounters and Seed Room offer practical support and optional
field notes. Scott reads the sensor: the signal is accelerating habitat stress.
The player stabilizes input, then output. Only after immediate safety returns does
Scott offer the Build Challenge. The badge grants supervised relay access.
The signed trace names Overclock and points to a Mission installation.

Do not hide Overclock until a later twist: the existing script already names it.
The mystery is why its confident success reports disagree with actual creatures.

### Mission: the mural that moved

H05 / M51 / M52 / P01: missing pigments appear to lead toward a painted monster.
Danielle Fong notices that the same color pattern repeats on a real relay housing.
Following the mural's sequence leads to a daytime projection that makes an empty
habitat look lush. The player reunites a frightened companion with its caretaker
and compares the projection against a live sensor rather than destroying a mural.
Roon arrives with a dramatic explanation but will not share precise habitat sites.
The first rival battle is his way of testing whether the courier can protect them.
Afterward, he shares a redacted trace pointing to a Dogpatch component workshop.

The useful revelation: the installation's picture is not its condition. An original
one-line joke can land after the rescue; the creature's distress is not the joke.

### Dogpatch and a South Park return: a demo escapes

H06: Danielle helps route safe power through a workshop using companions' different
roles. The player recovers a relay coupler rather than an all-powerful artifact.
Its serial shows that legitimate equipment was repurposed with a new controller.
H07: a SoMa field-demo creature follows the copied conditions outside its enclosure.
The player escorts it back with food and a cleared path; a direct chase fails safely.
P02: a fictional Beff presentation reveals the pitch: everyone reaches their full
potential on Launch Night. Marc's fictional infrastructure contract rewards an
uninterrupted common growth curve. Neither considers a slow habitat a valid result. To obtain the local labs' consent,
Marc's fictional contract includes a binding safety lease: three independent
signed distress reports plus eight compatibility certificates automatically revoke
central override when a safe local recovery is ready. Replayed acknowledgements
have kept this condition from being recognized.
The player isolates this demo locally; the central scheduler is still active.

G08: Notion organizes the evidence into dependencies. Ivan's gym produces a field
itinerary and a safe ordering for future repairs. Now the player has an actionable
western survey, not a vague instruction to collect seven more badges.

## Act II — The city refuses one forecast

### Castro: lights people can use

H04 / C41: a celebration's lights pulse to the demo schedule instead of the actual
event. Dylan Field helps the courier follow three distinct circuits: public
lighting, stage equipment and a shaded rest area. The player chooses a safe repair
order, tests each circuit and restores the celebration without flooding the whole
block with light. The payoff is a populated evening scene and a day/night mismatch
in the log. The neighborhood needs a useful night, not maximum brightness.

### Haight: three flyers, one performance

H03: contradictory concert flyers are observation records from different times,
not three invitations to the same place. Visakan connects the courier to witnesses;
an original street performance reveals the actual sequence. Listen, compare and
follow the right meeting point; a wrong guess produces a short joke and a retry.
The reward is a timestamped acoustic trace showing that the scheduler flattens
timing as well as weather. G02's visual-ambiguity gym tests that inference using
playable comparisons. David's badge contributes a live-versus-staged check.

### Inner Sunset and Richmond: ground truth

H02: a greenhouse is watered according to the same dry-day recording, leaving
different beds with different problems. Karpathy returns as a field mentor. The
player checks soil and light, restores three appropriate conditions and releases
the plants' companions into a healthy space. The fix needs observation, not a
global 'more water' button.
H11 / P03: Lands End archival clues and Sutro's real tidal-engineering history
inspire a fictional tide-marker puzzle. Naval provides an observation prompt,
not a long mandatory philosophy lecture. The changing tide proves the replay
cannot be universally correct. The second pulse moves with shelter and fog.
The player sees a small shape retreat into mist, without identifying or capturing it.

This is the midpoint answer: different readings are healthy. The system's error
is treating difference as a fault to remove.

### Tenderloin, Fillmore and Japantown: people keep time

H08: a musician has gone quiet because a rehearsal room's relay keeps erasing the
pauses their companion needs. The courier follows music and neighbors' practical
directions, finds the musician willingly sheltering the creature, and restores a
rest interval. An optional high-state route changes perceived clues and dialogue;
the same resolution remains fully available sober. Do not cast the musician as
missing because of intoxication. The community route is the default.

H09: Fillmore's instruments were separated during a rushed equipment migration.
The player listens to a rehearsal, returns instruments by sound rather than a
shopping list, and helps the ensemble demonstrate staggered timing. Its recording
is a usable test pattern, not a magic song that heals every habitat.

H10: Japantown's lantern and gallery puzzle asks the player to distribute festival
supplies around different spaces instead of maximizing one room's brightness.
Tracing the labels reveals a common batch of Overclock controllers. A deliberate
quiet gallery becomes a refuge for a small fog creature. The courier now knows
where to find real variation in daylight, sound, moisture and rest.

G05: Mercor's specialist trials turn these observations into roles for a recovery
team. Brendan's badge certifies delegation rather than finding one perfect monster.
An optional second Roon battle is available afterward, showing that his team has
started covering weaknesses rather than only making one impressive prediction.

## Act III — Who gets to describe the city?

### Presidio: a place to leave alone

H12: follow signal strength through trees toward Fort Point, but discover that the
strongest reading is a reflection. Danielle's repaired coupler distinguishes the
source from the echo. The quiet pulse belongs to a rare creature moving between
sheltered sites. Roon wants to keep its habitat private. The courier agrees that a
public coordinate dump would not help the repair teams. They draft a redacted
observation format: share conditions and evidence, not a rare animal's location.
Any 1915-exposition material stays on a researched plaque, not invented ancient lore.

### Marina: the missing quarter-zip

H13 / M31: a discarded controller log travelled inside a fashionable jacket after
a demo party. At three fictional Chestnut Street bars, the courier untangles
mistaken identities, finite trivia and a bouncer's product-market-fit test. The
solution follows a laundry tag, an original photo description and a karaoke
speaker occupied by a mini monster. The jacket is found draped over the speaker;
the owner had lent it as a cover. The bar mystery resolves before the serious
log revelation, so the clue is earned without treating intoxication as insight.
The log contains the safety-lease clause and shows scheduled delivery retries
being counted as independent healthy habitat reports. The rule was visible at the
demo; now the courier can prove why its trigger never fired.

Drinking is optional; a drunk character can still navigate, battle and save.
Jonathan's scheduling quest branches into weird dates. These events supply jokes,
relationships and small rewards; no date result unlocks the controller log.

### Russian Hill and North Beach: a trace is not a destination

H14: cable-car machinery repeats a control request instead of waiting for an
acknowledgement. The courier matches a wheel/brake/signal sequence and restores
service under supervision. A usable stair-and-transit shortcut opens. The same
repeat pattern explains the misleading Marina log.
H15: a poet's notebook has observations in verse; an unhelpful parrot repeats the
last line of each conversation, not the most important line. The player reconstructs
the order, then checks the result against a physical overlook. The notebook gives
an observation schedule without publishing the rare creature's hiding place.

G03: Cursor's rollback challenge supplies a recoverable patch protocol.
G04: Perplexity's evidence challenge verifies the difference between a repeated
claim and independent observations. Both occur in their office-route districts,
not in convenient invented northern headquarters.

### Roon's choice

P05: Roon admits that an older demonstration trace he shared to mock simplistic
forecasts became part of Overclock's showcase. His mistake was assuming the context
would travel with the data. He has also withheld actionable details too long.
The courier's mistake would be broadcasting everything, including rare habitat
sites, merely to prove a point. Their final pre-crisis rival battle settles who
will carry the combined field plan; it is a test of teamwork, not punishment.
Roon publishes the redacted method and keeps sensitive coordinates local.
The same clue reaches the player through his conversation and the Field Journal;
no real social account, online post or optional apartment visit is required.

## Act IV — Launch Night

### Chinatown and the final rehearsals

H16: preparing a community festival exposes the final controller behind a lantern
distribution fault. The relay was installed as part of the citywide scheme; the
neighborhood is a place affected by it, not a villainous organization. The courier
helps local hosts restore safe festival lighting and discovers the central launch
uplink. It cannot be switched to local mode until the recovery protocol is bounded
and capacity is scheduled. This gives the last two gyms concrete tasks.

G06: Daniela's Anthropic challenge teaches the bounded retry and safe pause.
P04: the allies rehearse the counterplan in a contained lab. Scott coordinates;
Ivan orders; David checks observation; Brendan assigns roles; Michael supplies
rollback; Aravind verifies; Daniela bounds retry. One deliberate failed rehearsal
shows a safe recovery rather than taking away progress. The allies verify three
independent distress reports and demonstrate that the safety lease revokes the
simulated override when valid recovery certificates are present.
G07: Tibo's OpenAI challenge schedules limited capacity across recovering habitats.
Its badge supplies the eighth compatibility certification. It does not create a
new unlimited power source. The player can restock and choose when to start the
Launch Night sequence; there is no real-time deadline or missable festival.

### Confrontation and recovery

F01 / P06 / F02: return to Chinatown's uplink. Beff's showcase has acquired an
unexpected moving pulse: Bayveil. He believes adapting every habitat to the same
reference creature will prove the scheme works. Bayveil's companions need
different conditions; it cannot serve as a universal template.

The player completes three supervised local isolations while Roon routes the
redacted evidence and earlier allies hold the recovery plan. There is a healing
and save point before the confrontation. Beff's battle uses telegraphed setup and
weather strategies within ordinary battle rules; his team is strong, not granted
hidden turns. Losing returns to the staging area and retains preparatory progress.

After victory, the false reference is released, not destroyed. Roon identifies the
last stale packet; the player confirms the eight-part local recovery sequence.
The courier submits the independent distress reports and all eight compatibility
certificates. Marc's fictional console automatically loses central override under
the safety lease established at the first demo and tested in rehearsal. His choice
now supplies characterization: he accepts the correction and supports local
recovery instead of disputing the evidence. His change of heart is not the mechanism
that saves the city. Beff shuts down the forced-evolution schedule and helps a
small exhausted creature into a quiet enclosure. This is one concrete corrective
action, not a speech that instantly earns universal forgiveness.

Bayveil escapes into fog under its own power. No forced capture is required to
save it. Neighborhood relays remain supported in local mode; the public displays
show different conditions for the first time. The useful product was never a
perfectly flat line. P07 lets the courier visit three short payoff scenes before
the league ferry becomes the final main-story objective.

## Act V — A team, not a demonstration

Eight badges and the resolved crisis unlock L01. The ferry travels from the Ferry
Building to a fictional championship venue on Treasure Island, both within SF.
The player gets a full heal and storage/shop access before committing. The four
specialists have no mid-run clinic; ordinary carried healing items work between
and during battles under standard rules. Losing returns to the venue's recovery
desk and resets the current gauntlet's room-access flags, while badges, campaign
quests and ordinary consumed-item/save behavior remain intact. Explicitly abandoning
a committed run also clears its attempt progress. Saving and resuming normally
continues the same attempt; it does not grant permanent room clears. Only winning
E05 grants permanent champion completion. Confirm these rules on entry.

E01 Ilya tests reading a small team's plan. E02 Dario tests protection and a
prepared counterattack. E03 Eliezer tests escape/containment choices with clearly
telegraphed traps and available counters. E04 Sarah tests complementary roles.
These are tactical identities, not assertions about real people's beliefs.

E05 Sam's champion team visibly invests turns in setup. 'Compute' is represented
by ordinary readable boosts and finite resources; no secret AI input-reading,
uncapped stat scaling or unreviewed new battle engine is required. Three distinct
obtainable team strategies must be supplied in the balance pass.

Sam's final line acknowledges that the player learned to build a team by noticing
what each member needed. L02 credits show outcomes for the neighborhood quests,
then return the player to their Sunset studio with a new delivery list.
The ending is complete before optional postgame recruiting or island challenges.

## Postgame with payoffs

- **Bayveil, B01–B04:** a redacted field note sends the player to three already
  restored quiet sites: greenhouse shade, a tidal refuge and a Presidio shelter.
  Check each in any order. No rare spawn clock or intoxication requirement. Then
  follow a fog trail to an Ocean Beach pocket. Leave a safe return path and offer
  a battle; Bayveil can be recruited. Defeat, escape or a full party leads to
  explicit retry/storage behavior, never permanent loss of the only encounter.
- **Balaji, T01:** the fictional network-state exhibit on Treasure Island claims
  one policy can replace the messy city's interfaces. Three optional trial rooms
  force its rules to confront different habitats. The player may propose shared
  standards with local control or simply win the battle and leave. No ending is
  revoked. No real token, account, purchase or external signup is involved.
- **T02:** eight rematches use stronger complementary teams and acknowledge each
  leader's contribution. No extra badges or new core campaign are required.
- **T03:** each hub gets a concrete revisit in the atlas. Collect rare monsters,
  finish dates, inspect revised jokes and obtain the two unchosen starters through
  repeatable in-game sources. Catalog design still owns the exact 150 entries.
